# Chapter 1 — Islam (pp. 9–24) — working points

Sections: Why it is so named (9) · Meaning of the word "Islam" (10) · Reality of Islam (10) ·
Reality of kufr (13) · Harms of kufr (13) · Benefits of Islam (17)

## p. 9
- Chapter opens with Bismillah and lists its six sections.
- **Why it is so named:** almost every religion takes its name either from its founder or from
  the people/nation it arose among. Author's examples:
  - Christianity — named after its founder, Jesus (Isa).
  - Buddhism — after its founder, Mahatma Buddha.
  - Zoroastrianism — after its founder, Zarathustra.
  - Judaism — after the tribe of Judah (Yehuda) in which it arose.
  - Other religions' names follow the same pattern.
- **Islam is different:** its name points to no person and no nation. It names a *quality*,
  the one contained in the meaning of the word "Islam".
- The name itself shows: not invented by one man, not reserved for one nation, no tie to any
  person, country or people.
- Its only aim is to create the quality of "islam" in people.
- Truthful, righteous people of every age and every nation who had this quality were all
  "Muslim" … (sentence continues on p. 10)

## p. 10
- (cont.) … were "Muslim" then, are Muslim now, and will be in future.
- **Meaning of the word "Islam":** in Arabic it means obedience and submission. The religion is
  called Islam because it is obedience and submission to Allah.
- **Reality of Islam:** everything in the world follows a fixed law.
  - Moon and stars are bound by a mighty law and cannot deviate from it by a hair.
  - The earth turns at a set speed, on a set path and timing, with no variation.
  - Water, air, light and heat each follow their own rule.
  - Minerals, plants and animals: each has an appointed law by which it is born, grows,
    shrinks, lives and dies.
- **Man is no exception:** look closely and the human being too is subject to the law of nature.
  - Born by the rule set for his birth; breathes, takes water, food, heat and light by it.
  - Heartbeat, blood circulation, breathing: all bound by the same order.
  - Brain, stomach, lungs, nerves and muscles, hands, feet, tongue, eyes, ears, nose: each
    part does exactly the work assigned to it, in exactly the way assigned.
- This mighty law, binding everything from the greatest planets to the smallest particle of
  earth, is a law made by one supreme Ruler. All … (continues on p. 11)

## p. 11
- (cont.) Everything in the universe obeys that Ruler, because it follows the law He made.
  So the "religion" of the whole universe is Islam, since Islam means obeying God.
  - Sun, moon and stars are muslim; so is the earth; so are air, water, light;
    trees, stones and animals too.
- **Even the denier is muslim in his body:** a person who does not know God, denies Him,
  worships others or associates partners with Him is still muslim by nature and constitution.
  - His birth, life and death all fall under God's law.
  - Every limb and every hair of his body has Islam as its religion, since each forms,
    grows and moves by God's law.
  - Even the tongue he uses to voice shirk and kufr is, in itself, muslim.
  - The head he forcibly bows before others is born muslim.
  - The heart in which, through ignorance, he holds love and reverence for others is muslim
    by nature.
  - Reason: all these obey God's law; every movement of theirs is under it.
- **Now a second angle — man's two sides:**
  1. Like all other creation, man is bound by the mighty rules of nature's law and is
     compelled to follow them.
  2. Unlike them, he has reason: the power to think, understand and form an opinion …
     (continues on p. 12)

## (1943 p. 12 onward) Man's second side
- Unlike the rest of creation, man has reason: he thinks, judges, accepts one idea and rejects
  another, prefers one way over another, and can make his own code of life or adopt someone else's.
  In this sphere he is not bound by a fixed law; he has freedom of thought, opinion and action.
- So two sides: (1) muslim by birth, compelled like everything else; (2) in the free sphere, being
  muslim or not is his choice, and this choice splits humanity into two groups.
- **The first group:** recognises his Creator, accepts Him as Master, and obeys His law in his free
  choices too: a complete Muslim. Now he consciously obeys the God he was already obeying
  unconsciously.
  - His knowledge is true (he knows the One who gave him the power to know); his reason and
    judgement are sound; his tongue is truthful; his whole life is upright.
  - He is at peace with the universe, serving the One all creation serves.
  - He is God's khalifa (deputy) on earth: the world is his, and he is God's.

## Reality of kufr
- The other person is also born muslim and stays muslim in body, but uses his knowledge and reason
  to not recognise God and refuses to obey Him in his free sphere: a kafir.
- Kufr literally means to cover or conceal. He is called kafir because he has drawn a veil of
  ignorance over his own nature: born on the nature of Islam, every part of him works by it, the
  world around him runs by it, yet his mind is veiled; he thinks and tries to live against it.

## Harms of kufr
1. **Kufr is ignorance, the real ignorance.** Seeing the vast "factory" of the universe run day and
   night, seeing in man an unmatched creation assembled from coal, iron, calcium, sodium and the
   like, seeing engineering, mathematics and chemistry at work everywhere, yet not knowing the wise
   Maker behind it. Missing the first end of knowledge, he finds no straight path in any field:
   darkness at the start, darkness at the end.
2. **Kufr is zulm (injustice), the greatest one.** Zulm = forcing a thing against its nature. All
   things obey God; their nature is Islam. The kafir uses them against that nature: he seats idols
   of others' greatness, love and fear in a heart made for God's; he uses his limbs and the world's
   things against God's will. He wrongs everything, even his own self, all the time.
3. **Kufr is rebellion, ingratitude and betrayal.** Man owns nothing of himself: brain, heart, eyes,
   tongue, hands, and the usefulness of everything around him are God's gifts. Using them against
   the Giver is the worst rebellion.
   - Analogies: a servant who betrays the master whose salt he eats; an officer who turns
     government-given powers against the government. Man's ingratitude to God is far greater.
   - All provision, all authority, every benefactor's means come from God.
   - Even parents' great right rests on God: who put love for the child in their hearts, milk in
     the mother, the readiness in the father to spend his hard earnings and time on a helpless
     infant? God is the true Benefactor, King and Sustainer.
4. **Kufr does not harm God.** His kingdom has no visible end even through great telescopes; earth,
   sun, Mars and countless stars spin at His command; He owns everything, has no partner, needs
   nothing. Man's denial only prepares his own ruin.
5. **Kufr ends in failure.** Knowledge that does not know its Creator cannot know anything rightly;
   reason that errs about its Maker stumbles everywhere. Morals, civilisation, society, economy,
   government and politics all go wrong; disorder, bloodshed, usurped rights and oppression follow;
   his own life turns bitter.
   - In the Hereafter, everything he wronged becomes a plaintiff: heart, brain, eyes, ears, hands,
     feet, every hair; the land he lived on, unlawful earnings, wealth spent on the unlawful, things
     he seized, tools of his rebellion. God, the true Judge, will do them justice and punish the
     rebel with disgrace.

## Benefits of Islam
- The universe everywhere shows God's lordship; its perfect, unchanging order testifies to a mighty
  Ruler none can defy. Man's nature too obeys Him without knowing.
- **Freedom is a test.** God gave man knowledge, thought, the sense of good and evil, and a measure
  of free will: a test of reason, discernment and the use of freedom. No compulsion, since
  compulsion defeats a test.
  - Exam analogy: forced answers make an exam meaningless; ability shows only when answering
    freely. Right answer → pass and progress; wrong → fail and block oneself.
- **Who fails:** misunderstands his own nature and the universe, errs about the Creator, uses
  freedom to disobey; fails in knowledge, reason, discernment and duty; proves himself the lowest.
- **Who passes:** uses knowledge and reason rightly; recognises and accepts God though not forced;
  chooses good freely though able to choose evil; obeys though able to disobey. He saw, heard and
  judged correctly and followed his judgement: he knows the truth and bows to it. Success in this
  world and the next is his.
- **In knowledge and work:** knowing God, he knows the beginning and end of knowledge and cannot be
  lost.
  - Philosophy: explores the universe without drowning in doubt like the kafir philosopher.
  - Science: discovers nature's laws, hidden treasures and powers, and the best uses of everything
    in earth and sky, but God-consciousness stops misuse. He never thinks "I own these things, I
    have conquered nature, I will use science to loot, kill and dominate" (the kafir scientist's
    way). The more he learns, the stronger his faith and gratitude: "my Master increased my power
    and knowledge; I will use it for my good and all humanity's."
  - History, economics, politics, law: works as hard as anyone, but with a right outlook, right
    purpose and right conclusions.
    - History: true lessons from the past, real causes of rise and fall, avoiding what destroyed
      earlier nations.
    - Economics: earning and spending that benefit all, not some at the cost of many.
    - Politics: peace, justice and goodness; no person or group enslaving God's servants;
      government and its powers as God's trust for people's welfare.
    - Law: rights fixed justly; no one wronged.
- **Character:** God-fearing, truthful, honest. Sees God as owner of everything, himself not even
  owner of his own body; all a trust to be used by God's will and accounted for one day.
  - Keeps heart, mind, eyes, ears and tongue from evil; prefers hunger to unlawful food; never
    raises a hand in oppression or walks towards evil; never bows to falsehood even at the cost of
    his head; never meets a need by injustice; holds truth dearest and sacrifices for it; hates
    injustice and never sides with it from fear or greed.
- **Such a person is, in this world:**
  - most honoured: bows to none but God, asks of none but God;
  - most powerful: fears none but God, hopes from none but God; no power moves him from truth,
    no wealth buys his faith;
  - richest: not a slave of desire or greed; content with lawful earnings; spurns piles of unlawful
    wealth. Contentment is the greatest wealth;
  - most loved: gives everyone their due, does good expecting nothing;
  - most trusted: keeps trusts and promises, honest in every dealing because God sees.
- The author concludes such a character can never be humiliated, ruled or defeated, since no power
  overcomes the qualities Islam creates.
- In the Hereafter: God's blessings, for he fulfilled the trust and passed the test with full
  marks; an unbroken success from this world to the next.
- **Islam is man's natural religion,** belonging to no nation or country. God-knowing, truth-loving
  people of every age and land followed it; they were Muslims, whatever their language called it.
