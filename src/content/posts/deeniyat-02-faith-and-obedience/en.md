## In brief

Chapter 1 showed that Islam means obeying God. Chapter 2 asks what makes that obedience possible. Maududi's answer: knowledge held with certainty, which is what *iman* (faith) is. Faith is the seed and obedience is the tree. Since our own reason cannot reach this knowledge with certainty, God sent it through His Prophets, and trusting them is the same kind of trust we place every day in a good doctor.

## 1. What obedience depends on

You cannot obey someone you are not sure of. Maududi names four things a person must know, and be certain of, before obedience to God can take root:

| One must be certain of… | Because… |
|---|---|
| **That God exists** | No one obeys a being whose existence he doubts. |
| **His attributes** | If you know He is One, you will bow to no one else; if you know He sees and hears everything, you will hold back from disobeying Him. |
| **His law** | You need to know what pleases and displeases Him, and to be sure that this law is His. Without that knowledge there is nothing to follow; with doubt there is no steady following. |
| **The Hereafter** | If death is the end, obedience and disobedience look equally pointless. Only certainty of judgement, reward and punishment makes a person bear the effort of obedience and give up tempting wrongs. |

Doubt is the enemy here. People commit fully only to what they are *sure* will help them, and avoid only what they are *sure* will harm them.

## 2. What iman is

That knowledge, held with certainty, is **iman**: to know and to accept. The one who has it is a **mu'min** (believer), and its natural result is that he becomes a **Muslim**, one who obeys.

Maududi's image is the **seed and the tree**. Without a seed there is no tree at all. But a seed can be planted and still grow weak, because of poor soil or a hard climate. In the same way, someone may truly have faith yet fall short in practice, through weak will, poor upbringing or bad company.

## 3. Four kinds of people

| | Faith | Conduct | Their standing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Yes | Fully obedient | **True Muslims.** They avoid what God dislikes the way one keeps a hand out of fire, and do what He loves as eagerly as one works for a living. |
| 2 | Yes | Falls short | **Still Muslims, of a lower rank.** When they disobey they are offenders who deserve punishment, but not rebels, because they still accept the King and His law. |
| 3 | No | Outwardly good | **Rebels in reality.** Good deeds without faith are not obedience to God, just as a man who rejects the king is no loyal subject merely because he hasn't yet broken a law. |
| 4 | No | Bad | **The worst:** rebels who also spread corruption. |

The lesson: everything rests on faith. Islam, whether complete or incomplete, grows only from the seed of iman. Where there is no faith there is kufr, which is rebellion against God, lesser or greater.

## 4. Where this knowledge comes from

The universe is full of signs of one wise Maker. Yet human reason, left to itself, has misread them again and again:

- some believed in two gods, three, or countless gods;
- some divided God's work among a god of rain, a god of wind, a god of fire;
- about life after death, some said death is the end, while others believed in being reborn in this world over and over;
- and working out a whole law of life by reason alone is harder still.

Even the finest mind, after years of study, could reach only an opinion, never certainty. Had God left people with nothing but their reason, only a few would ever have found the truth.

Instead, **out of His kindness**, God chose people from among humanity, gave them true knowledge of Himself, His law and the Hereafter, and told them to pass it on. These are the **Prophets**. The way God gave them this knowledge is **wahy** (revelation), and the book that contains it is **the Book of God**.

So the test now is different. When we look at a Prophet's pure life and lofty teaching, do we recognise the truth and accept it? Whoever does has passed. Whoever refuses shows that he has lost the ability to recognise truth, and he will never gain sound knowledge of God, His law or the Hereafter.

## 5. Faith in the unseen (iman bil-ghaib)

We already live by this principle every day:

- When we fall ill, we don't treat ourselves. We go to a doctor, trusting his qualifications, his experience and the patients he has cured, and we take his medicine as he tells us.
- In legal trouble we follow a lawyer; in study, a teacher; on an unknown road, someone who knows the way.

In every matter, we are bound to trust someone who knows what we don't. That is **faith in the unseen**: taking from a knowledgeable, trustworthy person what we cannot find out ourselves.

We cannot see God's being and attributes, the angels who carry out His commands, the right way to live by His will, or the reality of the Hereafter. We learn all of this from the Prophet, whose truthfulness, honesty, God-consciousness, pure life and wisdom assure us that what he says is true. That is why faith in the unseen is necessary for obeying God: without the Prophet there is no sound knowledge, and without sound knowledge no one can walk the path of Islam properly.

## Key terms

- **Iman:** to know and to accept, with certainty.
- **Mu'min:** one who has iman.
- **Muslim:** one who obeys God; obedience is the fruit of iman.
- **Wahy:** revelation; the means by which God gives knowledge to His Prophets.
- **Kitab Allah:** the Book of God, which contains that revelation.
- **Iman bil-ghaib:** faith in the unseen; accepting from a trustworthy source what we cannot know by ourselves.

## Points to reflect on

- Which of the four certainties (God, His attributes, His law, the Hereafter) feels weakest in us, and how might we strengthen it?
- In the seed-and-tree picture, what is the "soil" in our lives that helps or hinders our faith from growing?
- We trust doctors and teachers every day without a second thought. What would it mean to give the Prophet ﷺ at least the same trust?
