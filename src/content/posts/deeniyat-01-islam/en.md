## In brief

Maududi opens the book with one simple idea: *Islam* means obedience to God, and everything in the universe is already living that way. The only creature with a real choice in the matter is the human being. This chapter explains that choice, what is lost by refusing (kufr), and what is gained by accepting (Islam).

## 1. Why is it called "Islam"?

Most religions are named after a person or a people: Christianity after Jesus, Buddhism after the Buddha, Zoroastrianism after Zarathustra, Judaism after the tribe of Judah. Islam is named after neither. Its name describes a **quality**: submission and obedience to God.

This tells us two things:

- Islam was not invented by one man and does not belong to one nation.
- Anyone, in any age or land, who had this quality was a *Muslim*.

## 2. Everything already submits

The sun, moon and stars keep to their courses without the slightest deviation. Water, air, light and heat follow their own rules. Plants and animals are born, grow and die by the laws set for them. Even the human body breathes, digests and circulates blood by the same unbreakable order.

One law, made by one Ruler, governs all of it. Since Islam means obeying God, the "religion" of the whole universe is Islam. Even a person who denies God is, in his body, muslim: his heart beats and his lungs breathe in obedience to God's law.

## 3. The two sides of the human being

1. **The compelled side.** Like everything else, we are bound by the laws of nature and have no say in them.
2. **The free side.** Unlike everything else, we can think, judge and choose. We can follow God's guidance in our chosen actions, or make our own rules.

On the free side, humanity divides in two:

- The one who recognises his Creator and obeys Him by choice becomes a **complete Muslim**. He now obeys consciously the God he was already obeying unconsciously. He is in harmony with the universe and is God's *khalifa* (deputy) on earth.
- The one who uses his reason to deny God and refuses to obey Him in his chosen actions is a **kafir**.

## 4. Kufr: covering the truth

*Kufr* literally means "to cover". The kafir has drawn a veil of ignorance over his own nature. Born on the nature of Islam, in a world that runs on Islam, he still thinks and lives against it. Maududi lists its harms:

| Kufr is… | Because… |
|---|---|
| **Ignorance** | It sees the vast, intelligent order of the universe but misses its Maker. Knowledge without its first principle never finds a straight path. |
| **Injustice (zulm)** | Injustice means forcing something against its nature. The kafir uses his heart, limbs and the world's resources against the will they were made to serve. |
| **Ingratitude and rebellion** | Everything a person has, including the love parents feel for a child, comes from God. Turning His gifts against Him is worse than a servant betraying the master who feeds him. |
| **Self-harm, not harm to God** | God's kingdom is beyond measure and He needs nothing. Denial only prepares the denier's own ruin. |
| **Failure in both worlds** | Corrupt knowledge leads to corrupt morals, society, economy and politics. In the Hereafter, everything he misused will testify against him. |

## 5. What Islam gives

**Freedom is a test.** God gave us reason, a sense of right and wrong, and freedom to choose. That is what makes it a real test: an exam where the answers are forced proves nothing. Whoever chooses obedience freely, though able to refuse, has passed.

**Knowledge with direction.** Someone who knows God knows where knowledge begins and where it leads:

- in **philosophy**, he explores without drowning in doubt;
- in **science**, he discovers nature's laws but will not use them to plunder and dominate, and every discovery deepens his gratitude;
- in **history, economics, politics and law**, he works as hard as anyone, but aims at justice, the common good, and government as a trust from God.

**Character.** Seeing everything, even his own body, as a trust from God, he guards his heart, eyes, ears, tongue and hands; prefers hunger to unlawful food; never bows to falsehood; and holds truth dearer than any gain.

Maududi describes such a person as the most **honoured** (he bows to none but God), the most **strong** (he fears none but God), the most **rich** (contentment is the greatest wealth), the most **loved** (he gives everyone their due) and the most **trusted** (he is honest because God sees). He is successful in this world, and in the next he is rewarded for having kept God's trust.

**Islam is the natural religion of humanity.** It belongs to no single nation. Every truthful, God-conscious person in history followed it, whatever name their language gave it.

## Key terms

- **Islam:** submission and obedience to God.
- **Muslim:** one who submits to God.
- **Kufr / kafir:** covering the truth / one who covers it.
- **Fitrah:** the nature God created in everything, which is Islam.
- **Zulm:** injustice; using something against its nature.
- **Khalifa:** God's deputy on earth.

## Points to reflect on

- If our bodies already obey God without our choosing, what does it mean to obey Him in the part of life where we *do* choose?
- In which areas of life do we act as if we owned what we have, rather than holding it in trust?
- Maududi says contentment is the greatest wealth. Where in our lives do we need it most?
